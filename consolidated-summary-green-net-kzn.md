## A. KwaZulu-Natal municipal budgets, 2026/27 (Sources 1 & 7)

### Source 1 — South Africa Today, 7 July 2026 (article datelined Pietermaritzburg)
- KwaZulu-Natal Finance MEC Francois Rodgers officially welcomed improvements in municipal financial governance for the 2026/27 cycle.
- Per KZN Treasury assessments: of the **49 local and district councils** that tabled/approved financial plans for 2026/27, **43 are certified fully funded**, in line with Section 18 of the Municipal Finance Management Act (MFMA).
- Sharp improvement vs prior year: after the **2025/26 adjusted budgets, 19 municipalities had unfunded plans**; for **2026/27 that number dropped to six**.
- The six municipalities with unfunded 2026/27 budgets: Mpofana; iMpendle; iNkosi Langalibalele; eMadlangeni; Ulundi Local Municipality; uThukela District Municipality.
- Pending/undetermined statuses:
  - **eNdumeni Local Municipality** — status undetermined because the council had not yet officially approved its budget at assessment time.
  - **Amajuba District Municipality** — council informed Treasury it adopted its budget on 29 June 2026; funding status under assessment.
  - **iNkosi Langalibalele Local Municipality** — although listed among the unfunded, it re-tabled its 2026/27 budget on 25 June 2026; the revised submission is still awaiting reassessment.
- Rodgers cautioned that passing unfunded budgets is unlawful — a direct contravention of the MFMA and its regulations — undermines provincial financial stability and compromises day-to-day service delivery. Quote attributed to him: "We welcome the improvement in funded budgets, reflecting stronger compliance and financial discipline. However, unfunded budgets remain unlawful and undermine service delivery. The Government of Provincial Unity will continue to act against non-compliance while supporting municipalities to improve."
- The KZN Treasury reaffirmed commitment to partnering with municipalities on financial management frameworks and encourages open communication so every municipality develops credible, fully funded budgets.
- Article notes the accompanying image is AI-generated for illustration.

## B. KZN Growth Fund Agency (KZNGFA) — mandate and requirements (Sources 2, 3, 5)

### Source 3 — kzngrowthfund.co.za homepage
- The agency's brand essence centres on the concept of **financing growth in the province**.

### Source 2 — kzngrowthfund.co.za/projects/
Project requirements as listed:
- Projects must have a sustainable economic impact in KwaZulu-Natal.
- Minimum **40% BBBEE ownership** or a BBBEE score of at least 65 points (level 4).
- Funding is for **capital expenditure (CAPEX) projects**; funding is cash-flow based.
- BEE shareholders must be operationally involved in the business.
- Projects must stimulate sustainable job creation and equitable distribution of wealth within the KZN economy.
- Equity contribution of the promoter subject to cash flows and capital structure (**minimum 10%**).
- Projects must be at an advanced stage of implementation — disbursement within 6 months of application.
- **Working capital is not financed.**
- Portfolio/projects listed on the page: iDube Cold Storage; Dark Fibre Africa initiative (DFA); Southern African Shipyards (Pty) Ltd; Link Africa; Mpact; HBM SA Health Protection Services; Microfinish; Ross Healthcare (Pty) Ltd (Hillcrest Private Hospital and Gateway Private Hospital); Inoxa Industries; KwaDukuza Private Hospital.
- Corporate details from the page footer: nature of business project finance; Reg. No IT1437/2007/PMB; established August 2008; address 3rd Floor, Kingsmead Office Park, South Towers, 2 Kingsmead Boulevard, Stamford Hill, Durban; stated target clients "entrepreneurs, corporate, BEE groups."
- Site lists vacancies/opportunities including Chief Investment Officer (June 2026), two Investment Officers (June 2026), and a 24-month Internship Program (January 2026); annual reports up to 2024/25 are published.
- The page also displays an anonymous fraud/whistleblowing reporting channel (Vuvuzela Hotline: 0801 000 541) — site boilerplate, not a research finding about any project.

### Source 5 — KZN EDTEA provincial government page
- KZNGFA vision: to be KZN's leading Development Financier and Impact Investor.
- Core functions / mandates ("INSPIRED INVESTING"): accelerate Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment (B-BBEE), job creation, and unlock economic growth opportunities in KZN.

## Conflicts / tensions across sources

1. **Topic mismatch (note title vs contents):** the note is titled "Green Net KZN funding round investment," but none of the 10 sources mentions anything called "Green Net." Only source 8 concerns green energy, and it is about UPL's R17bn pledge, not a "Green Net" entity or funding round.
2. **Unfunded-budget counts:** source 1 gives both 19 unfunded (after 2025/26 adjusted budgets) and 6 unfunded (2026/27). These are different years/cycles, not a contradiction, but the raw figures differ and should not be conflated. Also, iNkosi Langalibalele appears simultaneously as "unfunded" and as "re-tabled, awaiting reassessment" within the same article — the article itself flags this nuance rather than resolving it.
3. **Two different R100.1bn references:** source 9 (Facebook post) and source 10 (kznonline) both cite R100.1 billion in pledges; source 10 dates it to the 2025 Trade and Investment Conference on 23 October 2025. They appear consistent, but neither states whether they refer to the identical event/figure pool — treat as corroborating but not independently verified.
4. **Distinct organizations with similar names:** "KZN Growth Fund Agency" (sources 2/3/5 — a development finance institution doing CAPEX project finance) vs "KZN Growth Network" (source 4 — an opportunities listing site). The note does not establish any relationship between them; do not merge them.
